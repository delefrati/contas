Before building on the Spotify Web API, settle the questions that decide how MB-16 and MB-17 are
built. Write the answers down; build nothing.

- **The app's quota:** a new Spotify app runs in Development Mode, open only to the Spotify
  accounts added to it by hand, and Spotify has tightened who gets extended quota. Check what
  the limits are today and whether minhabanda.com can live with them (every band's member who
  connects an account would have to be added), or what the alternative is.
- **Whose account owns the playlist:** each member connects their own Spotify, or the band keeps
  one shared account. Which one the playlist belongs to, and who can update it later.
- **The redirect on band subdomains:** Spotify accepts only exact redirect URIs, and each band
  has its own subdomain. Check that one callback on the central host, handing back to the band
  the way the Google sign-in does, works, and what the `state` must carry.
- **Tokens:** where the refresh token is kept and how it is encrypted at rest, with which key.
- **The scopes** the playlist needs (`playlist-modify-private`, `playlist-modify-public`), and
  nothing more.

**Done when:** a short write-up in `DEPLOY-OCI.md` (the Spotify app, its keys and redirect URI)
and in MB-16 and MB-17 records the answers, and those tickets are changed where an answer
changes them.

## Answers (2026-11-23)

From Spotify's developer pages, read on 2026-11-23: quota modes, the July 2026 quota update, the
February and March 2026 Web API changelogs, redirect URIs.

- **Quota:** Development Mode allows **5 users** per app, each added by hand by Spotify email,
  and the app owner must have Premium. Extended Quota is only for registered organizations with
  a launched service and at least 250k monthly active users. minhabanda.com can't get it, and 5
  users can't cover every member, or even every band.
- **Whose account (decided):** one Spotify account that belongs to minhabanda.com, connected
  once by a super admin, holds every band's playlists. It takes one of the 5 slots, for any
  number of bands. MB-16 connects it in the admin panel; MB-17 makes the playlists.
- **Redirect:** exact match, HTTPS, no wildcards, and no `localhost` (use `127.0.0.1`). Since the
  account is connected from the admin panel on the central host, one URI there is enough and no
  hand-off to a band subdomain is needed. The callback comes without the admin's token, so a
  signed, single-use, short-lived `state` naming the super admin proves it.
- **Playlists must be public:** Spotify's help says nobody can open a private playlist, even by
  link, so a band member couldn't open one in the platform's account. Public playlists show on
  the account's profile, so their names carry the band and the date, never the event's title or
  place.
- **Tokens:** the refresh token is stored encrypted with AES-256-GCM, keyed by a new
  `SPOTIFY_TOKEN_KEY`; the access token stays in memory. A refresh can return a new refresh
  token, which replaces the stored one.
- **Scopes:** only `playlist-modify-public`.
- **Endpoints after February 2026:** `POST /me/playlists` creates one (`POST
  /users/{id}/playlists` was removed); `PUT /playlists/{id}/items` replaces the tracks and `POST
  /playlists/{id}/items` adds them (`/tracks` was renamed to `/items`, not reverted in March),
  at most 100 per request.
- **Written up** in `DEPLOY-OCI.md`, section 8.
